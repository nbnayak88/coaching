## Scope and evidence
The source sheet contains 618 entries: 610 usable Drive links and 8 entries without a usable link. Titles include multiple editions and overlapping subjects. This plan is based on the title inventory, not a claim that the books have been read. Chapter mappings, page counts and edition applicability must be validated during intake. Download/copy completion is separate from learning completion.

## Commitment and feasibility
Baseline: 24 hours/week for 52 weeks = 1,248 hours. Across 618 entries that is only about 2 hours per entry before labs and course production. Therefore full expert mastery of every subject in a year is not a credible promise. The achievable target is an original synthesis and curriculum mapping for every available title, broad architectural fluency, and assessed applied mastery in priority tracks. Missing books remain explicit access dependencies.
Allocate weekly: 8h targeted reading, 8h experiments, 4h curriculum production, 2h spaced retrieval, 2h review and integration. Pilot for two weeks and adjust the scope against measured throughput.

## Weekly rhythm
Monday Workforce; Tuesday Revenue; Wednesday Finance; Thursday Procurement; Friday Supply Chain; Saturday Strategy and architecture; Sunday Source synthesis and review.
Weekdays: 2.5h/day = 12.5h. Saturday: 6h. Sunday: 5.5h. Total: 24h.
Each session: recall yesterday, ask a question, read selectively, test an idea, record evidence, identify the next uncertainty.
Process about 12 titles/week; reserve the final two weeks for the remaining entries and reconciliation. Twelve titles × 50 weeks = 600; 18 entries remain for reconciliation. This is synthesis throughput, not twelve complete deep reads every week.

## Book-to-course conversion workflow
1. Intake: stable B001–B618 identifier, source row, bibliographic edition, access status and file reference.
2. Diagnose: prerequisites, intended audience, business problem and three measurable outcomes.
3. Read: inspect contents, select relevant chapters, label skimmed versus deeply studied sections.
4. Experiment: hypothesis, synthetic inputs, steps, expected outcome, actual result and failure case.
5. Squeeze: write an original concise explanation and retain chapter/page references.
6. Build: place the lesson in a course, add a case, practice task, feedback and assessment.
7. Teach: deliver a 10-minute explanation without notes; capture learner confusion.
8. Validate: check recall after 1, 7, 30 and 90 days, plus transfer to a new case.
9. Revise: update misconceptions, prerequisites and release status.

## Squeeze specification
A squeeze means a 1–2-page original learning synthesis plus a 5–10-minute teach-back.
Include: problem; five essential ideas; process or architecture map; one worked example; one experiment; three misconceptions; five retrieval questions with rationale; chapter/page references; course and lesson IDs.
Use your own words and original scenarios. Keep full books private; public course materials should contain original teaching assets, not reproduced chapters.

## Course specification
Each course has 6 outcome-based modules: Discover, Explain, Configure/Build, Connect, Troubleshoot, Demonstrate.
Each module: 60–120 minute learning sprint, prerequisites, scenario, measurable outcome, guided lab, independent variation, evidence checklist and reflection.
Foundation: explain and navigate. Essentials: execute a guided scenario. Advanced: solve a new exception. Ultimate: defend trade-offs, teach and improve an integrated design.
Use Discover, Design, Build, Validate, Measure and Lead Labs as the recurring learning cycle; add other SuccessLabs labs when relevant.

## Mastery gates
Coverage: indexed and mapped; comprehension: explain without notes; application: reproduce a result; transfer: solve an unseen case; teaching: another learner succeeds from your material.
Suggested pass threshold: 80% delayed retrieval plus a complete independent lab, correct control checks and an architecture defense. Record rubric scores and observed weaknesses. Reading or AI-generated notes alone do not pass mastery.
